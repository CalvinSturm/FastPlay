FastPlay is a fast, lightweight native Windows video player built to open local videos quickly, seek responsively, use hardware-accelerated decode, and stay out of the way.

How to play videos faster on Windows

If your current player feels slow to open files or sluggish when you drag the timeline, the bottleneck is usually startup time and seek responsiveness rather than the file itself. FastPlay focuses on those two moments.

Open quickly

Double-click a file, drag and drop onto the window, or press Ctrl+O. FastPlay aims for a low open-to-first-frame latency so the picture appears fast.

Seek and scrub without waiting

Generation-based stale-work dropping means old frames never delay new ones. When you scrub the timeline or jump with the arrow keys, FastPlay drops outdated decode work and shows the frame you asked for.

Resume where you left off

FastPlay remembers a per-file position, so reopening a file picks up near where you stopped without building a persistent media library.

Keep playback smooth

Video stays on the GPU through a DXGI flip-model swap chain, and audio is decoded on an independent worker so sound stays realtime even when a heavy file falls back to software video decode.

FastPlay is designed for fast local playback. Final “fastest” claims require measured comparisons — see the FastPlay benchmarks page.
